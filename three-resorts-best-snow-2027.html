A strong El Niño tilts this winter toward Telluride

NOAA's Climate Prediction Center has an El Niño Advisory in force. Its 8 October 2026 discussion gives a strong to very strong El Niño a better than 83% chance of lasting through January–March 2027. El Niño pushes the winter storm track south.

Snow windows: late January, late February, early March

Ranking on snowfall alone points at December for Revelstoke, February for Telluride, and a December–March tie at Big Sky. Ranking on snowfall and base depth moves all three later, because the terrain that makes these mountains worth the trip — Revelstoke's 5,620 ft of vertical, Telluride's Gold Hill chutes and Palmyra, Big Sky's Big Couloir and North Summit Snowfield — is rocky and stays closed or thin on a shallow base.

Telluride: one week earlier keeps it refundable and under budget

Telluride town and Mountain Village are joined by a free gondola, running 20 Nov 2026 to 4 Apr 2027 from 6:30am to midnight. That single piece of infrastructure is why Telluride clears a brief that Revelstoke and Big Sky each half-miss: you can stay in a 19th-century mining town with a grocery, a gym and bars, and be on a lift in minutes without a car.

Big Sky: the best lift fleet of the three, the weakest town

Big Sky is the strongest of the three on lifts. The Lone Peak Tram rebuilt in 2023 carries 75 per cabin, Ramcharger 8 is among the fastest chairs in North America, and Swift Current 6 backs it up. Against that, “town” is Big Sky Town Center, a purpose-built commercial plaza rather than an old town — closer to a well-made outlet village than to Telluride or Revelstoke.

The zoning matters more here than at the other two. Mountain Village sits at the lifts; Town Center is a shuttle ride away; Meadow is further still. Two runs of the washer-filtered search for the March week returned 233 different listings between them, and only three within about a kilometre of the Mountain Village base come in under US$3,000 after tax. Many of the rest are in Gallatin Gateway and Bozeman, 30 to 55 km away.

Revelstoke: deepest snow, and the one resort that cannot do walk-to-lift

Revelstoke has the most snow days of the three — 93 against Big Sky's 70 and Telluride's 45 — and the largest lift-served vertical in North America at 5,620 ft. It is also the one that forces a choice against the brief.

Downtown Revelstoke is a real town, a working railway town with a grocery, gyms and restaurants. It is about 6 km from the resort. The lodging that sits at the base is essentially the Sutton Place hotel; the many newer Mackenzie Village and Mackenzie Plaza condos sit between the base and town, which their listings put about five minutes' drive from each. Nothing affordable is walkable to the Revelation gondola.

This is a milder version of the Jackson Hole pattern in the brief: great skiing, town not attached. The difference is scale — 6 km and a $2 bus against Jackson's 12 to 14 miles and 19 to 26 minutes by road — so it is a real cost but not a disqualifying one.

Correction: the resort bus is not every 15 minutes. The September version said so. BC Transit's current Route 5 timetable runs about every 30 minutes in the early morning and about every 45 minutes from late morning, at $2 a ride; the resort's page gives daily service from 6:15am to 8:30pm. No 2026-27 timetable had been published on 9 Nov 2026 — the newest notice is dated 13 Nov 2025.

Lift caveat: Revelstoke runs a short fleet — two gondola stages plus the Stoke, Stellar and Ripper chairs, per the resort's own hours page. Uplift capacity is low against Big Sky's tram-and-Ramcharger setup. The resort's 9 Nov 2026 pre-season news lists a new haul rope on the lower gondola and a new snowcat, but no new lift. In practice the 93 snow days and light crowds offset it, the same argument that kept Andermatt in play on the earlier page.

Short-term rental rules: the City of Revelstoke is on British Columbia's exempt list for the principal-residence requirement (list dated 1 Jun 2026); the surrounding rural Electoral Area B is not exempt. The pick, Revyverse and the Basecamp apartment each display a City of Revelstoke registration number on the listing, which places them inside the city; the other rows were not checked.

No single resort sweeps. Telluride wins on the brief as written and, relative to the other two, on this winter's outlook; Big Sky wins on terrain and lifts and is the weakest on lodging; Revelstoke wins on snow days and price and loses on lift access. Since the weeks do not overlap, the three remain complements rather than competitors.

Open checks before paying

  1. The cancellation line at checkout, on every listing. Seven of the fifteen policies read on 9 November refunded in full only for 24 hours after booking, with nothing on the search page to tell them apart.
  2. The Big Sky pick's walk. Ten minutes to the lifts is the host's figure, not a measured one, and the rating is the lowest of the three.
  3. Revelstoke prices are already USD. Add 16% British Columbia tax (8% PST, 3% municipal and regional district tax, 5% GST) and nothing else.
  4. The 2026-27 Revelstoke bus timetable, once published. On the current timetable the gap is 30 to 45 minutes, so a missed bus costs real time; parking or a rental car is the fallback.
  5. Snow at each refund deadline. 21 Jan for Telluride, 22 Jan for Revelstoke, 4 Feb for Big Sky. CPC updates its ENSO outlook monthly; the next is 12 Nov 2026.
  6. Passes. Epic for Telluride; Ikon or Mountain Collective for the other two, with the Big Sky tram billed per ride on either.
