The verdict

Engelberg, Switzerland — book this one.

It is the closest thing to Zermatt's shape at roughly half the price: 2,017 m of lift-served vertical (Zermatt is ~2,279 m), three glaciers, a top station at 3,020 m, and five genuine 576–1,959 m freeride descents — attached to a working town of 4,375 people with a Benedictine monastery, a Coop and a Migros in the centre, and a gondola 8–10 minutes' walk from the train station.

It is also the only resort on this list where the gym question has a clean published answer: Sporting Park, CHF 16 a visit, real squat rack, open 09:00–21:00 daily.

Weekly all-in for two, mid-January: about $2,350–2,750. Comfortably inside budget, with the cheapest walkable apartment at $914 for the week and free cancellation.

Runner-up on terrain, if you'll accept a tighter budget: Chamonix. Deepest terrain in the Alps and the only unambiguous town (pop. 8,705, tunnel freight, hospital, ENSA). Aiguille du Midi is a 8–10 min walk from Place Balmat and the Vallée Blanche is the single best day here. But see the Grands Montets finding below — it materially downgrades Chamonix for 2026/27 specifically, and the Mont Blanc Unlimited pass at ~$579 each eats your headroom.

Best value if the skiing wins outright: Alagna (Monterosa). Two 2,000 m lift-served itineraries off one lift, legal heliski, almost empty midweek — for $977–1,690 a week. The cost is that there is no gym within 36 km and the village is 430 people.

Best value if the town wins outright: Briançon. A real 11,411-person fortified town with Basic-Fit, Keep Cool (06:00–23:00), a CrossFit box, an E.Leclerc hypermarket and a night train to Paris — and apartments from $511 a week. The cost is that Serre Chevalier isn't actually steep.

Cut from contention on price: St Anton has arguably the best lift-served freeride in the Alps and a real town with a €46/week gym — but every in-village apartment with a washer priced €2,561–7,659 for your weeks ($2,971–8,884). It breaks the budget on accommodation alone. The cheap St Anton results are all down-valley in Pettneu, Flirsch and Kappl, which fail the walk-to-lift test.

Three findings that change the shortlist

1. Chamonix's Grands Montets summit is still closed — and will be for your trip.

The post-2018-fire replacement has slipped to December 2027, one season after you'd go. Grands Montets currently tops out at Bochard 2,765 m, not 3,295 m. The operator confirms it by omission: Mont Blanc Unlimited is sold as covering "Grands Montets (excluding summit)". The Argentière glacier classics — Pas de Chèvre from the summit, Rectiligne, the full 2,000 m top-to-bottom — are not lift-accessible in 2026/27. A new Argentière→Lognan gondola does open for 2026/27, so the lower half is freshly rebuilt. Vallée Blanche off the Aiguille du Midi is unaffected and remains the reason to come.

2. La Grave's cable car is a 50-year-old single point of failure that has already failed.

On 12 Dec 2024 a defect was found on a carrying cable; the replacement then went wrong when an anchor rated for 65 tonnes failed at 17, dropping 2.5 km of steel cable and damaging two pylons. The lift did not open until 15 March 2025 — two-thirds of the season gone. Winter 2025/26 ran normally. Separately, the planned third section to the Dôme de la Lauze was suspended by the Marseille court on 26 May 2026 over collision risk to the bearded vulture, so the ageing diesel Girose drag lift should still be the top link. If you book La Grave, refundable accommodation isn't a preference — it's the whole strategy.

3. Monterosa's best lift is not on the normal pass.

The standard Monterosa Ski pass excludes the Passo dei Salati → Punta Indren Funifor — the lift that makes Alagna a freeride destination. You buy an €8/day supplement at the lift, or the dedicated "Monterosa Freeride Paradise" product. The online shop literally sells multi-day tickets as "With Indren" vs "Without Indren". Budget ~€48 extra on a six-day trip and buy the right one.

Full ranking

Ranked against your stated priority order: (1) extreme terrain, (2) ease of getting from bed to lift, (3) a real town — then filtered by the $3,000/week all-in budget.

1

Engelberg, Switzerland

2,017 m lift-served3 glaciersReal town 4,375Gym CHF 16$914–1,305/wk

Terrain — the most lopsided pisted-vs-freeride ratio in the Alps

82 km of pistes with a single 4 km black run, versus five world-class freeride descents. For a serious skier the pistes are a warm-up and a transit network. The "Big 5", with official GPS verticals:

DescentVerticalTop → bottomAccess
Galtiberg1,959 m3,040 → 1,081 mKlein Titlis (Rotair)
Steinberg1,275 m3,039 → 1,764 mKlein Titlis
Laub1,194 m2,449 → 1,255 mStand → Laubersgrat chair
Sulz729 m2,500 → 1,771 mJochstock chair
Steintäli576 m2,507 → 1,931 mJochstock Express

Laub is sustained 30–40°. Galtiberg crosses the Galt Glacier and has two named killers — the Götterquergang traverse runs out over a cliff, and the Graben couloir is a dead end; transceiver/probe/shovel/airbag are listed as required and a guide is strongly recommended. Steintäli sits north and shaded behind the Jochstock, so it holds powder days after a storm — the day-4 run of a high-pressure week.

Real constraint: reaching Klein Titlis takes four long lifts (Titlis Xpress → Trübsee → Stand → Rotair), and the Rotair is shared with sightseers. Weekend mornings and midday queue. Midweek is close to essential — it's 30 min from Lucerne.

Yes. Population 4,375 (Dec 2025), up 48% since 1980 — a growing municipality, not a seasonal shell. Built around a working 12th-century Benedictine monastery, with Belle Époque hotels and a rail terminus.
Getting there
Zurich airport → Engelberg ~2h04 by train, one change. Lucerne–Engelberg 43 min.

Supermarket
Coop, Titliszentrum 5 — 217 m from centre, Mo–Fr 07:30–20:00, Sun 09:00–18:00. Sits between the centre and the Titlis station, so it's on your way home. Migros, Klosterstrasse 5 — 281 m.
6-day pass
⚠ CHF 365–390 estimated (~$449–480) — titlis.ch returned HTTP 429 on every attempt across two sessions. Day pass CHF 69 is confirmed for 2025/26. Not on Ikon, not on Magic Pass, no Epic. Verify on titlis.ch before booking. SBB Snow'n'Rail gives up to 20% off rail + pass and you'll arrive by train anyway.

January over March here, uniquely — Galtiberg ends at 1,081 m and Laub at 1,255 m, and those are the first things to go. Engelberg sits on the northern Alpine rim and catches the full brunt of northerly storms, which is why it over-delivers powder for its size.

March, clearly. Chamonix town is only 1,035 m — January valley snow is unreliable and it can rain in town. The Vallée Blanche is often not in condition in early January (crevasse bridges unformed, arête icy). Mid-March gives filled bridges, the handrail in place, long days, and the Grand Envers variants become realistic.

3

Andermatt, Switzerland

Shortest walk to lift (241 m)Legendary north faceOne cable car = one point of failure$910–1,500/wk

Terrain

Gemsstock 2,961 m − Andermatt 1,444 m = 1,517 m. Only 4 lifts and 31 km of pistes, of which 0 km is easy and 55% is difficult — there is literally no green or blue terrain on Gemsstock.

Correction worth knowing: the Bernhard Russi Run is not the off-piste north face — it's groomed black piste no. 71, 6 km and 950 m vertical over the Gurschen Glacier, patrolled and avalanche-controlled. The real north-facing bowl that "holds powder for days" has no named, marked, controlled itinerary. Treat it as backcountry; UIAGM guide strongly advised.

  • Felsental — the flagship, countless variations through gullies and hollows, 1,500 m to the valley station, accessed via the St. Anna Glacier.
  • Guspis — from the summit south, traverse to the Gafallenlücke (~80 m bootpack), then 2,944 → 1,468 m at Hospental: 1,588 m, 10.3 km. Initial slopes 40–45°, SE-facing. Bus or train back.
  • Giraffe — a steep gully; guides descend first as a safety demo before guests drop in one at a time.
  • Geissberg — lower section is steep, crusty and bushy at the end.

Two structural risks: the cable car is a single point of failure and a bottleneck — you queue in the village and at the mid-station — and if Gemsstock shuts for wind you're left with the mellow Nätschen/Sedrun/Disentis cruising. Disentis is linked by train, not lift.

Walk to lift & town

To the lift
Gemsstock valley station 241 m from the village centre; Gütsch-Express 437 m. Both genuinely walkable. This is Andermatt's best practical feature and the shortest walk of any resort here.
Real town?
Yes, unusually so. Population 1,527, a real municipality in Uri governed as an Offene Dorfgemeinde. A Swiss Federal Army garrison town since 1885, still an active training centre — that's why it has a street grid and year-round bones. But be honest about Andermatt Reuss: Switzerland exempted it from foreign-buyer restrictions to 2040 and from second-home caps, which is exactly the structure that produces an empty-in-shoulder-season quarter.
Gym
Andermatt Fitness (the Sport Studio, via the Andermatt Reuss car park) — 07:00–22:00 daily, strength and balance equipment, day and week passes sold to non-residents via Eversports. ⚠ Price not published. Alternatives: Radisson Blu Reussen gym (explicitly open to day guests) and The Chedi's 24-hour Technogym.
Supermarket
Coop, Gotthardstrasse 30 — Mo–Fr 07:30–18:30, Sa 07:30–18:00. 187 m from the village centre, ~400 m from the Gemsstock station. It is the only supermarket in town; big-format shopping is down-valley in Altdorf.
6-day pass
≈ CHF 380 (~$467) for the full SkiArena; Gemsstock-only day CHF 89. ⚠ Secondary source — the official ticket pages still served 2024/25 content. Epic Pass: Andermatt is on it, but the day count and 2026/27 terms could not be verified (Vail's queue system blocked every attempt). Worth resolving: Verbier is confirmed at 5 days on Epic, so one pass could underwrite a two-centre trip.

Mid-March on balance. The 1,444 m base is high for a Swiss village and the north aspect preserves powder all season, so you lose little by going later — and you gain daylight, stability on the 40–45° Guspis/Giraffe terrain, and far better odds the cable car actually runs. January is the better powder lottery ticket but carries more closure risk on a mountain where one lift failing removes your reason for being there.

Alagna / Monterosa, Italy

2,063 m lift-servedTwo 2,000 m itinerariesHeliski legalNo gym within 36 kmVillage = 430 people

Terrain — elite, and the emptiest on this list

Alagna 1,191 m; ski base 1,212 m; Punta Indren 3,275 m. Lift-served vertical 2,063 m. Access chain: gondola → Pianalunga 2,046 m → Funifor → Passo dei Salati 2,971 m → Funifor → Punta Indren. About 30 minutes village-to-glacier.

ItineraryVerticalNotes

Glacier access via Punta Indren, with Capanna Gnifetti (3,647 m) and Capanna Margherita (4,554 m, highest hut in Europe) onward. Heliski is legal and openly sold — Guide Alpine Alagna run it as a standard service; guided freeride from €80/person shared, €380 private. FWT Challenger runs here.

Honest downside: almost entirely guide-dependent, the Indren Funifor is a bottleneck, and if it's shut for wind your day collapses to ordinary intermediate pistes. Only 11% of the 132 km is graded difficult — the piste map is beside the point.

Walk to lift & town

To the lift
567 m, ~8 min from Alagna village centre to the Pianalunga gondola (pedestrian-routed). Champoluc: 736 m / ~10 min to the Crest gondola.
Real town?
No. Alagna comune is 728, but the village is ~430. Champoluc is a frazione of Ayas (comune 1,357); Gressoney-La-Trinité is 322. These are genuine 13th-century Walser settlements with their own Germanic dialect — but "year-round life" here means a church, a bar and a post office.
Gym
None. OSM returns zero fitness centres anywhere near Alagna. Nearest real gyms and full-size supermarkets are Varallo/Borgosesia, 36 km / 39 min. If a daily gym matters, Alagna fails your brief.
Supermarket
Carrefour Express, Località Miniere 9 — opened 26 Oct 2024 and was the village's first-ever supermarket. It's at the village entrance, ~1.0 km / 14 min from the cable car. Also Alimentari Bendotti Cristina, Via dei Walser 4b (centre, 07:00–20:00 daily) and Der Spicher. Champoluc has a Crai directly at the gondola base.
6-day pass
≈ €318–325 plus €48 of Indren supplements ≈ €370 (~$430). 2025/26 6-day was €312. ⚠ 2026/27 multi-day grid not yet published. Better buy: the Valle d'Aosta regional 6-day (€355 low / €386 high) covers Monterosa and Courmayeur, Cervinia, La Thuile, Pila and Skyway.

Lean March — a shut Indren Funifor guts the whole proposition, and it's a high, wind-exposed lift that runs more reliably later. Accept that the Balma/Malfatta exits down to 1,200 m will be spring snow at the bottom. January gives the least-tracked conditions of anywhere here; the place is genuinely empty midweek.

5

Courmayeur, Italy

Real town ~2,500Pedestrian Via RomaLow-season pass both weeksOnly 42 km piste, 5 blacks

Terrain — modest area, serious access

Cresta d'Arp 2,755 m, Cresta Youla 2,624 m, town 1,224 m, Dolonne 1,210 m. 18 lifts, 33 runs, only 5 black, 42.4 km of piste. Lift-served vertical is two numbers: 1,545 m counting Arp → Dolonne (which the operator lists under off-piste itineraries) or 1,414 m of actual pisted vertical.

  • Arp → Dolonne — 2,755 → 1,213 m, 1,542 m, NE-facing, many line options
  • Arp Vieille / "Vesse Couloir" — 1,114 m, north-facing, holds powder most of the winter
  • Arp → Val Veny (Zerotta) — 1,230 m
  • Skyway Monte Bianco → Punta Helbronner 3,466 m — Vallée Blanche to Chamonix (24 km), the Toula glacier back to Italy (2,000+ m), Marbrées. All mountaineering, all guided. ⚠ A live notice on the Skyway site says the Thoula staircase is closed — check before counting on it.

Verdict: a modest intermediate area attached to a genuinely alive town. Ski it unguided and you'll exhaust the serious terrain in two or three days. Budget for guides (collective freeride €155/pax; Toula €275) and the odd heli drop (from €390/pax, longest descent 1,750 m) and it becomes outstanding — plus Chamonix is a bus through the tunnel from €15.

Walk to lift & town

To the lift
Funivia Courmayeur 624 m / ~8 min from the south end of Via Roma. ⚠ The base is not on Piazzale Monte Bianco — it's ~600–750 m further south in the Pussey/La Villette quarter. Dolonne gondola 1,070 m / ~14 min. Skyway is 3.67 km — not a walk, take the Linea Blu bus. Bonus: the Funivia runs every evening to midnight, free with a 4+ day pass.
Real town?
Yes — ~2,500 (2,602 in 2022, slowly declining). Medieval core, genuinely pedestrianised Via Roma, parish church, ten inhabited hamlets, a 175-year-old guides' company, and a full summer economy (Skyway runs May–Nov with a botanical garden and a high-altitude winery).
Gym
Courmayeur Forum Sport Center, via Val Veny, La Villette — ~1.2 km / 16 min from Via Roma, beside the Dolonne gondola. 25,000 m² indoor: palestra e fitness, indoor climbing and bouldering, squash, tennis, padel, ice rink. Mon–Thu 09:00–13:00 / 15:00–19:00. ⚠ No published price list; no chain gym in town.
Supermarket
Carrefour Express, Via Circonvallazione — ~160 m / 2 min from Via Roma. Plus Super A&O (Strada Regionale 75, ~650 m), Carrefour Montagne and DPiù Discount at Entrèves. The real weekly shop is Famila, Morgex, 10 km / 13 min.
6-day pass
€323 (~$375) — and both your target windows fall in LOW season. High season 2025/26 was 20 Dec–6 Jan and 24 Jan–8 Mar, so mid-Jan and post-8-Mar are the cheap bands. Valle d'Aosta regional 6-day €355 is only €32 more and adds Monterosa, Cervinia, La Thuile, Pila and Skyway — for a strong skier facing only 42 km locally, this is the buy. Skyway return €59 separately, but a multi-day pass includes one free ascent.

Mid-January if you're coming for the freeride — snow-forecast's 2007–2026 record says the snowiest week in Courmayeur is week 2 of January, it's low-season pass pricing, and the lower mountain (1,210–2,256 m) faces east/south-east and bakes in March. Mid-March only if your priority is the high glaciers (Vallée Blanche, Toula, Brenva at 3,000–3,466 m).

Verbier, Switzerland

2,509 m spanGenuine freeride meccaPass published CHF 409Famous lines ski out in hoursAccommodation pricey

The tell that this is a real freeride destination: the headline expert descents are itineraries and off-piste, not pistes, and Mont Gelé has no pisted descent at all. Top Mont Fort 3,330 m; Le Châble 821 m. Le Châble → Mont Fort is a 2,509 m span — but that's span, not one run: realistic big descents are Mont Fort→Tortin ~1,280 m and Mont Gelé→Tortin 950 m over 4,300 m.

Named lines: Chassoure–Tortin (the "Tortin wall", north-facing, huge moguls), Gentianes–Tortin, Stairway to Heaven (north-facing, 10-min bootpack from Gentianes, holds cold powder into spring), Backside of Mont Fort (glaciated, long walk-out via Siviez), Vallon d'Arbi (cliff-exposed traverse), Col des Mines and La Marleinaz (south-facing, mornings only), and Bruson — the best tree skiing in the 4 Vallées.

Bec des Rosses, honestly: not a roped-off competition venue — the east side carries "a collection of other skiers' tracks" — but outside the patrolled area with no control, no patrol, no marked line, ~50–51° sustained with 55–60° entries, mandatory air and blind landings. A season-defining objective with a guide on the right day, not something you lap.

The real problem is crowding: Chassoure-Tortin and Mont Gelé "see so much skier traffic they might as well be marked as black runs." The famous lines get skied out within hours. The powder is in the traverses off them.
To the lift
Place Centrale → Médran ~550 m, 5–10 min uphill. Savoleyres ~850 m. The trap: many chalets sit on the upper slopes and are a real haul in ski boots — book between Place Centrale and Médran. Le Châble gondola runs 05:10–23:55, which makes a valley base genuinely viable and 30–40% cheaper.
Real town?
Semi. Verbier itself ~3,030 permanent, swelling to ~35,000 in winter — that >10× swing is the honest measure. The commune (Val de Bagnes) is 10,785, with Le Châble its administrative seat since the 12th century.
Gym
Centre Sportif de Verbier, Rue du Centre Sportif — salle de force, climbing wall, pool, squash, padel, ~250 m from Place Centrale. ⚠ No published drop-in price. Bring clean indoor trainers — marking soles are forbidden.
Supermarket
Coop, Rue du Centre Sportif — 256 m (same street as the gym); Migros, Route de Verbier — 353 m; Denner 366 m; Pam on Place Centrale.
6-day pass
4 Vallées CHF 409 (~$503) — published for 2026/27. Verbier–La Tzoumaz only CHF 374, but it excludes Mont Fort, and the delta is only CHF 35 over six days. Buy the 4 Vallées. 10% off online ≥10 days ahead → ~CHF 368. Also on Epic (5 consecutive days).

Mid-March, before the Xtreme. The south-facing aspect problem is real, but Verbier's marquee lines — Gentianes–Tortin, Stairway, Chassoure — are precisely the north-facing ones that survive March, and the Mont Fort glacier is at its best. ⚠ 2027 FWT Xtreme date not published; 2026 ran 28 Mar.

7

Briançon / Serre Chevalier, France

Best town by far — 11,411Basic-Fit, Keep Cool, CrossFitCheapest: from $511/wkNot actually steep

Honest verdict: not a freeride destination. 250 km and 82 pistes but only 13 black runs (13%), no glacier, and max continuous descent ~1,300 m. The independent read is blunt: terrain suits experts "due to extensive off-piste, though not particularly steep… accessibility over extreme difficulty, making it ideal for confident intermediates learning freeriding." Even the flagship Vallon de la Montagnole is described as "not too technical."

What it genuinely does have: the Vallon de la Montagnole (~1,000–1,100 m, due north off the Yret, exiting onto the black Tabuc), La Cucumelle flanks (over 1,000 m without meeting anyone), L'Eychauda bowls, and the Couloir de la Balme. And the real strength — tree skiing across roughly two-thirds of the mountain in larch, which sheds its needles so "you can see your line for hundreds of metres." Some of France's best terrain for a white-out. ⚠ The key lift is bad: the Yret is a 1984 fixed-grip triple, 16-minute ride.

To the lift
Prorel gondola base is 12–20 min walk from the Cité Vauban, and here's the detail nobody mentions: the old town sits at ~1,320–1,350 m and the gondola base at 1,212 m, so you walk down 120 m in the morning and back up 120 m in ski boots in the dark. Reaching the top of the Yret from Briançon is close to a 90-minute commute each way. Le Monêtier (5–12 min to the lift) is the better base if skiing wins.
Real town?
Yes, and it's the best here. 11,411 people, highest city in France, UNESCO Vauban fortifications, 24/7 A&E at Centre Hospitalier des Escartons, lycée and collèges, weekly market, cinema, casino — and a rail terminus with direct services to Marseille, Grenoble and Paris including night trains.
Gym
Keep Cool, 289 Rue des Tabellions — open 06:00–23:00, 7 days, €34.99/4 weeks. Plus Basic-Fit, 1 Rue du Moulin, CrossFit Onyx 1326, BeYou, ASPTT Musculation, a national altitude-training centre, and Bloc 027 climbing gym. No other resort here is close.
Supermarket
E.Leclerc hypermarket (7 Rue de la Soie), Intermarché (Chemin Fanton, 08:30–20:30 daily), Aldi (78 Rue des Couteliers), Carrefour Market, Carrefour City, Lidl, Biocoop. ⚠ None of the three ski villages has a full supermarket — only Sherpa-format supérettes.
6-day pass
€345 high season (~$401) — published for 2026/27, covers Briançon/Prorel. Plus five free partner days: one each at Alpe d'Huez, Les Deux Alpes, Puy-Saint-Vincent, Montgenèvre–Vialattea and Sestrières–Milky Way. La Grave is not included (you get −25% instead).
8

La Grave, France

Most serious mountain here — 2,150 m3-min walk to the liftCheapest: from $706/wk479 people, no gym, no supermarketLift failed for 3 months in 2024/25

Téléphérique des Glaciers de la Meije, built 1976/77: La Grave 1,470 m → Peyrou d'Amont 2,416 m → Col des Ruillans 3,173 m, with Girose glacier drags reaching ~3,550 m. Vertical 2,150 m — 2,300 m if you ski below the resort to the road.

No pisted runs, confirmed in the strongest terms: "no piste machines and no groomed slopes, apart from a single blue on the glacier"; "below 3,200 m there is no signposting, no grooming and no avalanche control." The operator states you are personally liable for any accident. Patrollers advise from a wooden chalet at the base and that is the extent of the safety net. Named terrain: the Vallons and Chancel itineraries, Trifides couloirs, Col du Lac. Beacon, shovel, probe mandatory; guide strongly recommended.

Read the cable-car warning in the findings above before booking anything here. The lift dropped a carrying cable in December 2024 and did not open until 15 March 2025. Refundable accommodation is the entire strategy.
To the lift
Trivial — the base station's address is literally "1 Place du Téléphérique, Le Village". Village supermarket to lift: 212 m / 3 min. Everything in La Grave is inside 5 minutes.
Real town?
No. 479 people in La Grave, 300 in Villar-d'Arêne. No nightlife beyond a handful of bars, no cinema, no shopping. A working hamlet on the N91 under the north face of the Meije. For a week this is either exactly what you want or unbearable.
Gym
None. Zero fitness facilities across both communes. Nearest is Briançon, 41 km / 45 min over the Col du Lautaret — which can close in storms.
Supermarket
Alp'Servit, 212 m from the téléphérique. Villar-d'Arêne's épicerie opens Mon 09:00–11:00, Tue & Thu 17:00–19:00, Sat mornings — which tells you the scale.
6-day pass
€330 (~$383). Day €66. Season €1,138; weekdays-only season €637. A 6-day+ pass also gets −25% at Serre Chevalier, Alpe d'Huez and Les 2 Alpes.

March. Holds cold snow better than anywhere here (north-facing off the Meije with the Girose on top), but January brings short days on a mountain where route-finding matters, a thin snowpack over the crevassed upper Vallons — and the historical fact that when this lift breaks, it breaks in December.

9

St Anton am Arlberg, Austria — best terrain on this list, priced out

1,507 m vertical, 200 km of marked powder routes alongside 300 km of piste, the Valluga north face and Bridge Couloir (40°, guide mandatory — confirmed officially, there's a crossed-out-skis sign at the cable car), Schindlerkar, Mattun, the Rendl backside (1,329 m over 4.5 km), and north-facing Albona at Stuben. Roughly double Livigno's or Arabba's snowfall (380 cm/yr, January peak 43 cm/week). A real town of 2,357 with a Railjet station in the centre, Arlberg WellCom gym at €11.50/entry or €46/week, and Spar + Billa on the main street. Walk to the Galzigbahn from Dorf; Nassereinbahn 5 min east.

Why it's ranked 9th and not 1st: every in-village apartment with a washer priced €2,561–7,659 for your weeks. The cheap results (Pettneu, Flirsch, Kappl, Gaschurn at $1,150–1,800) are all down-valley and fail the walk-to-lift test. Add a €468 pass each and it does not fit $3,000. If your budget ever moves to ~$4,500/week, this becomes the number one answer.
10

Saas-Fee, Switzerland — a ski-mountaineering trip mislabelled as a resort

Biggest vertical of the Swiss options (1,775 m, Mittelallalin 3,456 m), most snow-sure, and the only resort here where March out-snows January (45 vs 33 cm/week). Car-free, real village of 1,637. Coop on Untere Dorfstrasse. Aqua Allalin gym CHF 25 with pool (Nautilus machines, not free weights); CrossFit Saas-Fee inside the Walliserhof has platforms and competition barbells but published a closure notice — email first.

The disqualifier: crevasse danger genuinely limits off-piste, more than you'd assume. The published assessment is that on parts of the Feegletscher "the risk of ending your day (or your life) down a deep crevasse could be as high as 50:50." The glacier splits the ski area; the funicular mid-station glacier access can only be used with a certified guide who signs the party in; safe freeriding is ~20 km. The tell: the local freeride school drives clients to Zinal-Grimentz, Lauchernalp and Leukerbad for freeride days. A 52-year-old snowboarder died in a crevasse here on 2 March 2025. Go for the Allalinhorn with a guide, or don't go.
11

Tignes / Val d'Isère — most terrain, worst price-to-untracked-snow ratio

Grande Motte funicular lifts you 2,100 → 3,032 m in 6–7 min, then cable car to 3,456 m; Tignes vertical 1,906 m. Real lines: Vallon de la Sache (~1,200 m, 10 km, under a 3-min hike — but a wrong turn puts you above large cliffs), Les Tufs (nine couloirs, 45–53°), Couloir des Pisteurs (44°, 300 m, 45-min approach), Col Pers (~1,100 m, only 30–35° — the danger is the Gorges de Malpasset exit, not the pitch). Face de Bellevarde is 850 m at max 35.4° — a groomed World Cup leg-burner, not a technical test.

Val d'Isère is the better base: an 18th-century Baroque church, semi-pedestrianised centre with roads buried underground, and — the single best fact — the Solaise gondola and Olympique 3S both leave the same hub, the Rond-Point des Pistes, 5 min from the main street. Val Claret is 1968 concrete on a treeless shelf with no civic life. Tignes has the better gym: Le Lagon, €12.50 single / €65 weekly, with squat cages and free weights — and it publishes its prices, which Val d'Isère doesn't.

Why it's low: €468 6-day pass — the most expensive here — plus $1,636–4,471 accommodation, mostly without free cancellation. And it tracks out brutally fast: the accessible classics are skied out by lunchtime on day one, it's almost entirely treeless, and you should budget two or three lost days to flat light.
12

Les Deux Alpes, Ischgl, Livigno — each fails one priority hard

  • Les Deux Alpes — huge numbers (2,243 m range, 2,121 m in one go, new Jandri 3S to 3,200 m in 17 min) and the cheapest big-resort accommodation here ($724–1,220). But 65% of the piste map is graded easy, the extreme terrain is glaciated and guide-dependent, the resort is purpose-built on a ~2–2.5 km strip, and I could not confirm a named gym exists — a new pool/ice complex is advertised for winter 2026. Call +33 4 76 79 22 00. Best partner deal on this list though: a 6-day pass gives 4 free partner days plus 2 at Alpe d'Huez.
  • Ischgl — near-identical vertical to St Anton (1,512 m), superb lifts, three gondolas walkable in a pedestrianised village, cheapest Austrian pass (€385). Real steep terrain exists (Piz Val Gronda's ~900 m into the Fimbatal, Palinkopf ski routes 36/39, Lange Wand at 633 m and over 70% gradient) — but the core of the mountain is wide intermediate cruising, it gets 30% less snow than St Anton, and the only gym (Silvretta Therme) has no free weights, no dumbbells, no barbells, no squat racks. Also: essentially no in-village Airbnb inventory with a washer — every result was down-valley.
  • Livigno — 982 m of lift-served vertical, which is the killer number: two-thirds of St Anton's. Only three itineraries exceed 40° and none is longer than 1,000 m. Dry continental snowpack with persistent weak layers, and January daily maxima below freezing (mean −12.5/−4.8 °C). It has the best gym of any resort here by a distance — AQ1816, a CONI Olympic preparation centre at 1,816 m with a real free-weights area — plus duty-free shopping and legal heliski from €1,200/flight. But as a freeride destination the verdict is that "demanding steep-slope junkies" won't find it here.
13

Arabba — 275 people and no gym

Three genuinely good north-facing blacks straight to the village (Fodoma 2.8 km at max 56%, Sourasass 3.3 km / 749 m, Ornella Variante), the Marmolada "La Bellunese" — 12 km and ~1,823 m from Punta Rocca 3,269 m (a groomed red, long and scenic rather than hard), and guided access to Val Mesdì, the most famous freeride descent in the Dolomites (1,200–1,500 m through a limestone canyon off Sass Pordoi). Lifts leave directly from the village.

But: 275 permanent residents, only 14% difficult terrain, the second-driest January here (20 cm/week), no gym at all — the nearest is Dòlaondes in Canazei, 15 km over the Passo Pordoi — one small Despar, and four bars. It will be quiet at night. Choose it for pistes and scenery, not off-piste.

What to verify before booking

  1. Engelberg's 6-day pass price — titlis.ch returned HTTP 429 on roughly a dozen attempts across two sessions. The CHF 365–390 figure is inferred from the typical Swiss day-rate multiple, not published. This is the highest-priority follow-up since Engelberg is the recommendation.
  2. Andermatt's Epic Pass terms — blocked by Vail's Queue-it waiting room. Verbier is confirmed at 5 days on Epic; if Andermatt's terms are similar, one pass could underwrite a two-centre trip for near the price of one week's local pass.
  3. La Grave's cable car and the Girose téléski — confirm both are scheduled to run for 2026/27 before committing.
  4. Your Monterosa pass must be the "With Indren" variant, or budget €8/day at the lift.
  5. In-unit vs shared washers on any Airbnb you shortlist — the search filter is unreliable at the listing level (see caveat above the accommodation table).
  6. Gym drop-in prices at Chamonix, Verbier, Andermatt and Courmayeur — only Engelberg, St Anton, Tignes and Saas-Fee publish theirs.
  7. Les Deux Alpes: does a gym actually exist? A new pool/ice complex is advertised for winter 2026 but no named facility could be confirmed. +33 4 76 79 22 00.
  8. Chamonix MBU 6-day — the ~€499 is arithmetic from the announced +3.98% increase; the full 2026/27 grid isn't published. The 1-day at €104 is confirmed.
