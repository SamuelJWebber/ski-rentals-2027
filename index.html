Steep skiing with a real town, for under $3,000 a week

For winter 2027: Engelberg in Europe, Telluride in North America, Hakuba in Japan. Each pairs serious lift-served vertical with a town you can walk across. St Anton has the best terrain of any of them and works only if you relax the in-apartment washer or take a 10-night stay. If fast lifts matter more than small-town character, Whistler scores on all four criteria at once.

Researched 10 to 11 September 2026 for 1 to 3 people, for the weeks of 9 Jan, 16 Jan, 23 Jan, 6 Mar and 13 Mar 2027. Ranked on a strict priority order: terrain first, then walking distance to a lift, then a real town with a gym, a supermarket and life after skiing, and finally lift speed. Every price was a live quote on the date researched. Re-check before booking.

Priority 1Extreme terrain. Steeps, couloirs, glaciers, big lift-served vertical. Not groomers.
Priority 2Walk to the lift. Not a shuttle chain, not a drive.
Priority 3A real town. Gym, supermarket, wifi, life beyond the resort.
Priority 4Fast lifts. Detachables and gondolas, not 1970s fixed-grip doubles.
The benchmarkZermatt's shape at a lower price. Jackson fails on the town, not the mountain.

The answers

Europe · best terrain, with one condition

St Anton am Arlberg

Tyrol, Austria · a Railjet station in the village
$2,837village median week for two, without a washer
  • The washer is the deciding factor, and it breaks the budget. Three-quarters of village apartments have no washing machine at all, and adding one costs 52 to 64% more. No Saturday-to-Saturday week for two with a confirmed in-unit washer comes in under $3,000 in any of the three target weeks. For three people it is not close, starting at $4,664.
  • The one option that works. Studio Stanton, $2,600 with a confirmed in-unit washer, 200 m from the Nassereinbahn and ski-in ski-out, next to a bakery open from 6am. The catch is that it books only as a 10-night block, 14 to 21 January or 11 to 21 March, never Saturday to Saturday. If your dates can flex off the Saturday changeover, this is the answer. Its cancellation terms could not be checked, because it never appears in the main search grid.
  • The near miss. Sonnenalp Apartment 1 at $2,775 for 9 to 16 January, a 10-minute walk from the Galzig and Rendl lifts, carries a free-cancellation badge and lists a washer among its own appliances with no shared laundry anywhere on the page. That is suggestive but not proof, and it has no dryer and no gym.
  • Terrain. The best in the European audit. Schindlerkar, Mattun, the Valluga and Rendl, across 300 km of piste on one pass.
  • Lifts. Rated 5 out of 5, with 145,948 people an hour. The 2019 Schindlergratbahn is a heated 10-person gondola moving 2,800 an hour directly onto the steeps.
  • Walk to the lift. 367 m, about five minutes, to the Galzig Funitel. Better still, the pistes end at the lift bases, so you ski home to your door.
  • Town. 2,296 residents, three supermarkets, and the Arlberg WellCom centre with an outdoor 25 m pool and a fitness studio. The train station is a seven-minute walk, so you can arrive from Zurich with no car and no transfer.
  • The catches. A queue spike from 8:45 to 9:30 at the Galzig, which locals beat by taking the 8:40 bus to Zürs. Lech and Zürs need a bus. Add the tourist tax of €5 per person per night. And March costs more than January here, because Easter falls on 28 March 2027.
Europe · take 9–16 January

Engelberg

Obwalden, Switzerland · 45 min from Lucerne by train
$2,350–2,750all-in for two, including lodging, pass and food
  • Terrain. 2,017 m of lift-served vertical against Zermatt's 2,279 m, off a 3,020 m top station over three glaciers. The Galtiberg descent runs 1,959 m. The Laub is 1,194 m of sustained 30 to 40 degrees. The Steintäli holds powder days after a storm.
  • Town. 4,375 people, a working Benedictine monastery, Coop and Migros in the centre. The gondola is an 8 to 10 minute walk from the railway station.
  • Gym. Sporting Park, CHF 16 a visit, with an actual squat rack.
  • Lodging. From $914 a week with free cancellation, available on all four target weeks.
  • The catch. 82 km of piste with one black run. The pistes are transit between freeride laps, not the point.
  • Lifts. Rated 5 out of 5, tied with Zermatt and St Anton. Village to the 3,020 m summit in about 30 minutes over three rides. The Laub and Steintäli run on 1996, 2002 and 2019 detachables. A second summit line opened in 2026.
  • Why January. The Galtiberg finishes at 1,081 m and the Laub at 1,255 m. Too low to trust in mid-March.
North America · January only

Telluride

Colorado, USA · Ikon
≈$2,100all-in, week of 23–30 Jan 2027
  • Terrain. Gold Hill chutes and the Palmyra Peak hike-to terrain, genuinely steep and avalanche-controlled inbounds.
  • Town. The only North American resort where the lift is in the town. Lifts 7 and 8 launch off the town grid and Clark's Market is on Main Street. The gondola to Mountain Village is free, 12 to 13 minutes, running 6:30am to midnight and to 2am on Friday and Saturday, from 20 Nov to 4 Apr.
  • Lodging. A verified 5-minute walk to Chair 7 at about $2,100 all-in.
  • The catch. Both March target weeks are Telluride's declared peak for lodging and lift tickets. The same units run $3,100 to $3,800, a swing of $1,300 to $1,600.
  • Lifts, honestly. Ranks 6th of 9 here. The mean lift dates from 1997, and the town chairs are fixed-grip doubles from 1975 and 1972 that will still be running. Town grid to the top of Gold Hill is 30 to 45 minutes over three or four lifts. But that is a once-a-day commute: the Gold Hill and Prospect pod then laps at 3:39 to 5:06 on detachables.
  • March substitute: Jackson, not Crested Butte. Crested Butte fails on closer inspection. Its queue is short but unavoidable, since every lap of the extreme terrain needs the 1992 Silver Queen first, the North Face T-bar shuts at 3pm and the High Lift usually opens only in January. Jackson in March runs to 11 April and ranks second in the United States.
Japan · January or March

Hakuba

Nagano, Japan · stay Happo village or Wadano · Ikon
$1,950–2,600all-in including two 6-day Hakuba Valley passes at $410 each
  • Terrain. The only Japanese mountain here with sustained steep lift-served skiing: 1,071 m of vertical, 37 degrees maximum, with Omusubi and Black Back averaging 27 degrees, plus real alpine backcountry above the Grat quad.
  • Town. Happo and Wadano are walkable to the lifts with shops and restaurants. The valley pass covers ten resorts and the shuttle is free on ski days.
  • Lifts, honestly. The weakest fleet audited: 72 to 75% fixed-grip, eleven pair lifts at 2.5 m/s, not one bubble or heated chair. Omusubi and Urakuro, the two reasons to go, are both served by 1,000-per-hour pair lifts. The fast quads serve the groomers.
  • Bad timing. 2027 is the final winter of the Adam gondola. The base ski school and summit terrace are already demolished, and the double-capacity replacement opens December 2027. Going a year later gets a materially better resort.
  • Why still Hakuba. Transit is fast despite the fleet: about 12 minutes for 1,060 m of vertical. Niseko has far better hardware but weaker steeps, a 500-per-hour single chair guarding its peak, and four shuttle-separated areas.
If fast lifts rank first or second

Whistler Blackcomb

British Columbia, Canada · Epic
$1,950–2,700all-in, lodging for two with 16% BC tax
  • The only resort scoring on all four. It is not the steepest, the smallest or the cheapest, but nothing else here clears every bar at once.
  • Lifts. First by a distance. 71,761 people an hour, more than Telluride, Hakuba, Jackson, Crested Butte, Fernie and Revelstoke combined. Thirteen detachable chairs including an 8-pack and four 6-packs at 3,600 an hour, eight gondolas, and the Peak 2 Peak tricable. Only 24% fixed-grip, and four lifts new or replaced since 2022.
  • Terrain. Whistler Bowl, Couloir Extreme, Spanky's Ladder, Blackcomb Glacier and the Peak-to-Creek fall line. Deep and varied rather than Palmyra-steep.
  • Town. Walkable in ski boots, full grocery, gyms, wifi, a year-round community. Creekside is the quieter alternative with its own 2022 gondola.
  • The cost. The biggest crowds here, with village gondola queues on weekends and powder mornings. You trade Telluride's intimacy for roughly three times the uphill capacity.

The lift audit

Fixed-grip share alone mis-ranks resorts, because a mountain can own sixteen slow beginner chairs and still run fast lifts where it matters. The two honest tests are the share of uphill capacity that is slow, and what type of lift serves the terrain you actually came for. On that second test the rankings shuffle hard.

What the audit changed

  • Engelberg survives, and the worry that prompted this was wrong. I suspected the Rotair was an old pulsed cable car creating a bottleneck. It is a 2014 Garaventa tram carrying 75 people at 925 an hour, running continuously at 10 metres per second, and it is the newest lift in the chain. Two of the three signature descents never touch it.
  • Chamonix is eliminated. The Aiguille du Midi departs every 15 to 30 minutes with a 59-skier cabin limit, a practical throughput of 120 to 240 skiers an hour. The Grands Montets summit stays shut until winter 2027/28.
  • Andermatt and Verbier drop, and for the same instructive reason. Both spent heavily on lifts, and both spent it on the cruising terrain. Andermatt's ski arena rates 5 out of 5 while the Gemsstock, the only reason a steep skier goes, is capped at 650 an hour on 1990 and 1991 trams with officially acknowledged queues. I called Andermatt "clearly out" earlier and that was too strong: the documented waits are 35 to 40 minutes on a holiday morning and around 20 to 30 minutes on a busy Sunday, not the hour-plus its reputation implies, and midweek reports are consistently queue-free. A sweep of every Andermatt mention in the main Alps skiing forum found no wait-time figure at all, which suggests the longer numbers people repeat cannot actually be sourced. The separate reason to hesitate is size: regulars describe the Disentis half as "a lot of so-so short runs" and warn it may be "a little small for one week." Verbier's 2021 base gondola moves 3,200 an hour into trams from 1981, 1983 and 1986 carrying 550 to 1,050. Both fixed the bottom and pushed the constraint uphill.
  • Zermatt's expert terrain has quietly lost its lifts. The Gornergrat to Stockhorn tram was dismantled in 2007 and the Triftji and Stockhorn T-bars closed in 2017. What remains is Rote Nase, a 1986 tram at 520 an hour, now a guide or instructor only zone running only on the hour. A replacement gondola is "being considered" with no date. Budget CHF 180 to 200 for a guide.
  • Big Sky's tram is a per-ride charge for Ikon holders. Between $10 and $40-plus per ride, priced dynamically and billed to a card held on file. Under the old daily fee you paid once and lapped; per-ride pricing makes repeated Big Couloir laps cost-prohibitive.
  • Jackson adds lift reservations for 2026/27 for all Ikon and Mountain Collective passholders. The bus is verified at 31 minutes express and 44 minutes local, so your instinct about the distance was about right for the local service.
  • Jackson handles crowds better than Crested Butte, despite the famous tram line. Independent scoring puts Jackson at 8 out of 10 for crowd flow against Crested Butte's 5, and second in the United States against thirty-first. The reason is redundancy. Jackson's tram is 500 to 650 an hour and the only way to Corbet's, so a storm day means an hour in line and the busiest days a few hours. But you can skip it entirely and lap Thunder and Sublette, both rebuilt in 2022 and 2024, which roughly halved their ride times. Two caveats on the tram: it runs its last trip up and down at 3:30pm, and it shuts when summit winds pass about 70 miles per hour, which also closes the upper mountain. The resort's own president has conceded that lines run long on weekend powder days despite ticket caps, because season passholders are never capped. Crested Butte has no such option: every lap of the extreme terrain needs the 1992 Silver Queen first, and Vail removed its designated wind backup in 2019 without replacing it. Jackson's worst queue is long but avoidable; Crested Butte's is short but unavoidable.
  • Crested Butte's problem is uptime, not crowds. Typical waits are zero to fifteen minutes. But the Silver Queen has gone down for four and a half days at the start of spring break, the High Lift slid backwards fifty feet after a brake failure in 2024, the North Face T-bar was "down a significant amount of the time" in one reviewer's visit and closes at 3pm, and the High Lift usually opens only in January. A fully approved 500-acre expansion with three chairlifts, including a replacement for that T-bar, has sat untouched since June 2019 and appears nowhere in Vail's 2026 capital plan.
  • Fernie's expert terrain is barely open. Polar Peak, its only alpine lift and the access to its best double-black terrain, opened 11 days in 2017/18 and under 10 days in 2018, against a comparable lift at Whistler running 98% of the season. Reviewers report it closed for entire week-long trips. Getting there takes three lifts and 23 minutes of riding, two of them fixed-grip. Its two new detachable quads, which halve the base ride and replace the three-lift exit from Cedar Bowl with one, open in winter 2027/28, a year after this trip.
  • Low lift capacity cuts both ways on freeride terrain. The same 650-per-hour cap that creates Andermatt's queue also limits how many people reach the summit, and reviewers report untracked powder within a five-minute traverse of the top station ten days after the last snowfall. Courmayeur's 1963 cable car to Cresta d'Arp, moving 360 an hour, draws the same comment: "like your own private freeride lift." If you ski midweek, a slow lift onto serious terrain can be a feature. It is only a defect when you are queueing behind a weekend crowd.
  • Two Andermatt specifics worth knowing. The Gemsstock and Gütsch lift bases are not connected to each other, so crossing between them means a bus. And Epic Pass holders do not get the Matterhorn Gotthard railway included, which removes the standard workaround of taking an early train to a higher station to skip the Gemsstock queue.
  • Monterosa carries a wind risk worth pricing in. Nothing is planned or under construction anywhere in the area. Regulars report losing anywhere from zero to more than half a week to wind holds on the Alagna side, and a ticket seller was recorded warning buyers off six-day passes. Champoluc and Gressoney are the safer bases.

Six findings that changed the shortlist

  • Zermatt is more affordable than assumed at this scale. Apartments with a washer priced at $1,268 to $2,435 for 9–16 January and $1,491 to $2,556 for 6–13 March, mostly with free cancellation, with a six-day pass at CHF 384, about $480. Zermatt is expensive by the month; for one week with two people it largely fits. Nothing beats it on shape, and several places beat it only on price.
  • The Jackson objection is directionally right but the distance is wrong, and there's a better answer inside it. Teton Village is 12 to 14 miles and 19 to 26 minutes from Jackson, not 40. More useful: Snow King is a lift inside Jackson town, six blocks and about a thirteen-minute walk from Town Square. Treat it as a warm-up, an evening session or a storm-day backup rather than a ski area for this brief: it gets 150 to 200 inches against the big mountain's 458, you can ski it out in under two hours, its gondola actually moves about 1,000 an hour rather than its 2,000 design figure, and it is on neither Ikon nor Mountain Collective, so it costs $100 to $110 a day on its own. The real case for Jackson is staying in the town and taking the $3 bus, 31 minutes on the express, to a mountain ranked second in the United States.
  • Chamonix's Grands Montets summit is still shut. The post-fire replacement has slipped to December 2027, one season after this trip. It tops out at Bochard, 2,765 m, and the operator sells Mont Blanc Unlimited as covering "Grands Montets (excluding summit)". The Argentière glacier classics are not lift-accessible. The Vallée Blanche is unaffected.
  • St Anton has the best terrain here, and the washing machine is what keeps it off the top. Two research passes disagreed by a factor of two, and both were wrong. The first quoted the right numbers in the wrong currency. The second searched an area spanning 30 km and several other ski areas, and was contaminated by a carousel of listings Airbnb labels "Available without a washer." Bounded to the actual village, the median week for two is $2,837 without a washer. Three-quarters of village stock has no machine, and adding one costs 52 to 64% more, so no Saturday-to-Saturday week for two with confirmed in-unit laundry lands under $3,000 in any target week. One property beats it, Studio Stanton at $2,600, but only as a 10-night block.
  • Snowbird and Alta are out despite the best lift-served steeps in the United States. No town, no supermarket, no gym, and interlodge lockdowns you are not legally allowed to leave. On-mountain lodging starts around $3,864 all-in.
  • Two lift traps. Monterosa's Punta Indren lift, the whole reason to ski Alagna, is not on the standard pass: it costs €8 a day extra or needs the "With Indren" product. And La Grave is not included on the Les Deux Alpes pass, only 25% off a day ticket. The cable car link between them was suspended by a French court in May 2026 over a bearded vulture collision risk and was still stopped in July. Plan as if it does not exist.

Which week

13 to 20 March is the cleanest week in Europe. The French school zones are back by 8 March, the Swiss holidays are done, and Easter falls unusually early in 2027 on 28 March, so late March is not the usual quiet bet.

Two exceptions. Take 9 to 16 January for Engelberg, because the Galtiberg ends at 1,081 m and the Laub at 1,255 m, too low to trust in mid-March. And Telluride is January only, since both March weeks are its declared peak for lodging and tickets.

In Japan, mid-March is roughly a third of mid-January for the same unit, and the Coming of Age Day weekend on 9 to 11 January adds no premium. One caveat specific to 2027: it is the final winter of Hakuba's Adam gondola. The base ski school and summit terrace are already demolished, and the double-capacity replacement opens in December 2027, so the same trip a year later is a materially better one.

Two research traps worth knowing

Airbnb quotes in US dollars by default, whatever country you search. Verified by loading the same Whistler listing twice: CAD 3,060 and US$2,212 for the same week, a ratio of 1.383. An earlier version of the rentals report divided the Canadian figures by 1.38 and understated Whistler, Tremblant and Sun Peaks by about 38%. That is now corrected. If you re-run any of these searches, do not convert the numbers.
Airbnb's washer filter is unreliable in two distinct ways. It matches building laundry as if it were in-unit, including paid machines. And the filtered results page appends a carousel headed "Available without a washer" whose listings a scraper reads as matches. Airbnb also uses three different labels, and a bare "Washer" means the location is unspecified, not that it is in your apartment. Only "Free washer, in unit", or a laundry-room photo alongside the kitchen, actually confirms it.
Airbnb's town searches reach much further than the town. A search for St Anton returned 683 homes spanning Pettneu, Flirsch, Kappl, Ischgl, Landeck and Gaschurn, up to 30 km away and in different ski areas entirely. Bounding the map to the village itself left 40. Any median taken from the unbounded search understates a resort village badly.

Verify before you commit

  • Engelberg's six-day pass. Published third-party figure is CHF 274, about $338, for the full Titlis area; the CHF 213 figure on the resort site covers only the small Brunni and Klostermatte sector, not the Titlis freeride terrain. The official ticket site rate-limited every attempt and neither page is labelled with a season, so confirm at purchase. This is the recommendation's weakest number.
  • In-unit laundry on any specific listing, on the listing page rather than from the search filter.
  • Whether Fernie town actually has a gym, if you go there. The municipal aquatic centre explicitly has no weight room, the replacement field house will not be open for this season, and no commercial gym appears in any directory reachable from here. Check by phone or on a map before relying on it. The town is also 8 km from the resort by road, about 12 minutes, not the 5 km every guide quotes, and the shuttle costs $5 each way through an app with no cash sales.
  • Telluride's gondola dates if you travel at the edges of the season. It runs 20 Nov to 4 Apr. Its replacement is stalled over federal funding rules, and the Lift 7 and 8 replacements approved in June 2026 may themselves be fixed-grip, which the town objected to.
  • Whether the Big Sky tram surcharge applies to you. Ikon and Mountain Collective holders pay $10 to $40-plus per ride, billed automatically. Day-ticket, Gold and Double Black holders ride free.
  • Jackson is on the full Ikon Pass only, not Base and not Session. Check which one you hold before planning around it. New for 2026/27, Ikon and Mountain Collective holders must reserve each ski date in advance and the resort warns days may sell out. Reservations are non-transferable and cancel by 11am on the day.
  • Jackson's free head start. Ski patrol opens Teewinot and Apres Vous thirty minutes before the other upper-mountain lifts, which costs nothing and beats the tram queue. The paid alternative is an Early Tram upgrade sold in limited daily quantities through the ski school by phone, at an unpublished price.
  • Jackson parking if you drive. Base lots run $35 to $45 on peak days, the Ranch Lot is free with four people in the car, and the Stilson lot seven miles out is free with a free shuttle running from 5:30am to midnight. Winter parking passes went on sale 22 September 2026.
  • Do not stay in Teton Village if the town criterion matters. It has the lifts at its door and a population of 517, but no supermarket, no public gym, and the road through the national park closes from November to mid-May, making it a dead end all winter. The nearest full supermarket is eleven miles away.
  • Cancellation tier at checkout. Airbnb prints the exact date there. Firm gives you 30 days, Limited 14, Moderate 5, Flexible 24 hours. All show the same "free cancellation" badge.
